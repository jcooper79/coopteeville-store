# Competition & Market Check (Nov 2026)

## Short answer
**There is a market, but it is crowded and commoditized.** Car-humor tees clearly sell: several dedicated stores exist, and marketplaces are full of them. But nearly everyone sells the same thing (a catalog of jokes aimed at hardcore "car guys") and competes on ads and price. Coopteeville is differentiated **only if the characters become content people follow.** As just a catalog, it's one more shop.

## Nearest competition

| Competitor | What they are | Who they target | Gap Coopteeville can use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **100MPH** (100mph.cc) | Dedicated car-humor and "car culture" tee store, about $30 a shirt | Gearheads, garage humor | Insider jokes for enthusiasts; no characters, no community |
| **Aggressive Thread** | Car-enthusiast tees (turbo, diesel, JDM) | Modders, racers | Same: niche insider humor, catalog model |
| **365carmods** | Funny "car guy" tees | Manual-transmission and mechanic crowd | Same |
| **Amazon / Etsy / Redbubble / Fine Art America** | Endless print-on-demand sellers | Everyone, mostly price-driven | No brand, quality varies, race to the bottom |
| **Threadless** | Community-voted designs (weekly submissions and voting, top picks printed) | Art and design fans | Proves voting works; not car humor, no character world |
| **Hoonigan** | Car-culture lifestyle and media brand | Enthusiasts and fans of its videos | Shows media drives apparel; parent company filed Chapter 11 in Sept 2024 and much of its on-camera team left |
| **Licensed animated-car merch** (big studio films) | Kids' licensed apparel | Kids and parents | Kids only, licensed. We target adults and must avoid that look (see BRAND.md) |
| **Custom plate sellers** (Etsy plates, a German plate-tee listing on eBay) | Custom metal plates, a few plate tees | Gift buyers | **No polished, branded plate-tee maker found.** Weakest competition of any of our features |

## Where Coopteeville is actually different
1. **Audience: everyone who drives, not just car people.** Every competitor writes for enthusiasts (boost, JDM, manual swaps). Our residents joke about traffic, parallel parking, the check engine light, range anxiety, and the minivan life. That audience is many times bigger, and it's the **gift-buyer** market (Father's Day, Christmas, birthdays, new drivers, retirements). **This is the sharpest positioning and should lead the marketing.**
2. **Characters, not a catalog.** No car-humor competitor has a cast. Characters are the only part a copycat can't clone overnight, *if* we invest in them (animated shorts, trading cards, recurring storylines).
3. **The Plate Maker.** Personalized plate tees are underserved, and every plate is a one-off people want to show off.
4. **Voting on designs.** Threadless proved the model in general art; nobody does it for car humor. It tells us what to print before we spend anything.

**Honest caveat:** each mechanic exists somewhere on its own. What's new is the combination: a character universe aimed at everyday drivers, with voting and personalization. The jokes alone are copyable within a week.

## Market size (directional only)
Published estimates disagree a lot, so use them as background, not a plan:
- Graphic-tee market: estimates range from about $2.6B (2025) to $25B (2028), depending on the definition.
- Custom tee printing, including print-on-demand: about $4.7B–$9B in 2025, growing 7–11% a year.
- Personalization and self-expression are the most cited growth drivers. That's good for the Plate Maker.

What matters more is the bottom-up math. These are scenarios, not forecasts:

| Orders a day | Average order | Revenue a year | Gross profit at ~35% after printing + fees |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | $40 | ~$73k | ~$26k |
| 20 | $40 | ~$292k | ~$102k |
| 50 | $45 | ~$821k | ~$287k |

Ad spend and returns come out of that gross profit. Most print-on-demand brands stall at a few orders a day because they rely on paid ads. Getting to 20+ a day almost always takes content that brings people in for free.

## Is "limited" a problem?
No. A tight concept is easier to remember and cheaper to market. Grow **inside** the theme rather than adding random categories:
- **More residents** (unlocked by Mileage Club or voting): minivan mom, tow truck, school bus, ice cream truck
- **More products that fit cars:** stickers, air fresheners, hats, mugs, kids' tees, all printed on demand
- **Gift-occasion collections:** "Dad's First Car", "New Driver", "Retired and Riding", "Road Trip Crew" matching family tees
- **Club Kits:** B2B for car meets, driving schools, dealerships, auto shops

## What would kill it
- Launching as a catalog with no character content. That makes us 100MPH with a cartoon.
- Bad print quality early on, which means bad reviews. See PRINT-SPECS.md and order samples.
- Leaning toward the big studio's animated-car look (legal risk).
- Spending on ads before a free post shows which hook works.

## How to validate cheaply before scaling (4–6 weeks)
1. Put the storefront live with Pit Lane voting and the Plate Maker working, plus 12 launch tees.
2. Post 3 resident shorts a week on TikTok, Reels, and Shorts. Track which resident and which joke takes off.
3. Spend $300–500 on ads only behind the best organic post. Measure cost per email and cost per order.
4. Success signal: **at least 2% of visitors buy, and at least 20% of buyers make a plate.** If plates drive buying, lead with them.
